Zipper math

The broken zipper in your hand has no size printed on it, the shop sells fixed lengths, and the one you bought is ten centimeters too long. Zipper math names the gauge from one measurement, picks the stock length your opening actually needs, and tells you exactly where to cut a long one down. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Gauge match - one measurement across the closed teeth names the size number.
  • Length plan - opening centimeters become the stock zipper to buy.
  • Shorten - a long zipper becomes a cut line and two top stops.

Labeled norms

Gauge: the size number is roughly the closed-chain width in millimeters - #3 = 3.5 mm, #4 = 4 mm, #5 = 5.5 mm, #8 = 8 mm, #10 = 10 mm. Uses: #3 suits skirts, dresses and pillows; #5 suits jackets and bags; #8 and #10 suit coats, tents and gear. Length: buy the next stock size up from the opening plus about 2 cm. Shortening: cut from the top at the target plus about 1.5 cm, one top stop per side. Labeled sewing references, not rules.
